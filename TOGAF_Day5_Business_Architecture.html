Phase B: Business Architecture

How the enterprise needs to operate: capabilities, value streams, the common steps of Phases B–D, and gap analysis

Phases B, C and D follow the same pattern: describe where you are (Baseline), describe where you want to be (Target), and work out the difference (Gap). Phase B does this first for the business, because every later domain exists to support it.

01Purpose of Phase B

Phase B develops the Business Architecture: how the enterprise needs to operate to achieve its business goals and respond to the strategic drivers set out in the Architecture Vision.

It describes the business strategy, governance, organization and key business processes, along with the capabilities and value streams that deliver value to customers.

02Objectives

  • Develop the Target Business Architecture that describes how the enterprise needs to operate to achieve its business goals, respond to its strategic drivers, and address the Request for Architecture Work and stakeholder concerns.
  • Identify candidate Architecture Roadmap components based on gaps between the Baseline and Target Business Architectures.

03The Common Steps for Phases B, C and D

Phases B, C and D share nine steps. Learn them once and apply them to every domain:

  1. Select reference models, viewpoints and tools
  2. Develop the Baseline Architecture Description
  3. Develop the Target Architecture Description
  4. Perform gap analysis
  5. Define candidate roadmap components
  6. Resolve impacts across the Architecture Landscape
  7. Conduct formal stakeholder review
  8. Finalize the architecture
  9. Create the Architecture Definition Document
Baseline or Target first? TOGAF allows either. If the change is large and the target is very different from today, it is often more useful to develop the Target first and describe only as much of the Baseline as is needed to find the gaps.

04Key Business Architecture Concepts

ConceptMeaning
Business capabilityA particular ability that a business may possess or exchange to achieve a specific purpose. It describes what the business does, not how.
Value streamAn end-to-end collection of value-adding activities that create an overall result for a customer, stakeholder or end user.
Business processA sequence of activities that shows how work is done; processes realise capabilities.
Business functionDelivers business capabilities, closely aligned to an organization but not necessarily governed by it.
Business serviceSupports business capabilities through an explicitly defined interface, and is explicitly governed by an organization.
Organization mapShows the main organizational units, partners and stakeholders and the relationships between them.
A capability is not a process. "Payment processing" as a capability is stable over time; the processes that realise it change as technology and organization change. Exam questions often test this distinction.

Useful techniques in Phase B

  • Business capability mapping and value stream mapping (see the TOGAF Series Guides)
  • Business Scenarios to discover and document business requirements
  • Business modeling: activity or process models (e.g. BPMN), use-case models and class models
  • Organization mapping and information mapping

05Gap Analysis

Gap analysis is used in Phases B, C and D to validate the architecture and find what must change. TOGAF's technique uses a matrix:

  • List the Baseline building blocks as rows and the Target building blocks as columns.
  • Add a final row, "New", and a final column, "Eliminated".
  • Where a block appears in both, mark it as "Included".
  • A Baseline block with no match in the Target is either deliberately eliminated or accidentally omitted. An accidental omission must be fixed.
  • A Target block with no match in the Baseline is a gap to be filled: something to develop or procure.
Gap analysis also highlights services that are potentially reusable, and is a powerful way to check that the Target Architecture is complete and consistent.

06Outputs

  • Refined and updated Phase A deliverables, such as the Statement of Architecture Work and validated principles, goals and drivers
  • Draft Architecture Definition Document, with the Baseline and Target Business Architecture and views addressing stakeholder concerns
  • Draft Architecture Requirements Specification, with gap analysis results and technical and business requirements
  • Business Architecture components of an Architecture Roadmap

The takeaway

  • Phase B develops the Baseline and Target Business Architecture and identifies candidate roadmap components.
  • Phases B, C and D share nine common steps: reference models and viewpoints, baseline, target, gap analysis, roadmap components, resolve impacts, stakeholder review, finalize, and create the Architecture Definition Document.
  • Capabilities describe what the business does; value streams show how value is delivered end to end; processes show how work is done.
  • Gap analysis uses a Baseline × Target matrix with "New" and "Eliminated" entries.
  • Outputs: draft Architecture Definition Document, draft Architecture Requirements Specification, and Business Architecture roadmap components.
