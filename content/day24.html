Yesterday's scenarios covered developing the architecture. Today's cover delivering and governing it: Phases E to H, governance and Requirements Management. These are areas where Part 2 answers often hinge on following the governance process rather than taking a shortcut.

1. What Strong Answers Contain, Phases E–H

PhaseA strong answer usually mentions
ECorporate change attributes and business constraints · consolidating gaps, requirements and interoperability · dependencies · readiness and risk · the implementation and migration strategy · work packages · Transition Architectures that deliver value · the initial roadmap and plan
FFitting the plan to existing management frameworks (business planning, portfolio/project, operations) · business value of each work package · resource estimates · prioritization by cost/benefit and risk · finalizing the roadmap and plan · Implementation Governance Model · lessons learned
GArchitecture Contracts · compliance reviews with tailored checklists · the correct compliance level · Architecture Board involvement · time-bound dispensations with conditions · Change Requests · records in the Governance Repository
HMonitoring change drivers · analyzing the change's impact · classifying it (simplification, incremental, re-architecting) · the stakeholder rules of thumb · Requirements Impact Assessment · a new Request for Architecture Work where needed
Req. MgmtRecording the requirement in the repository · Requirements Impact Assessment on current and earlier phases · iterating back where needed · updating the ARS and, if scope changes, the Statement of Architecture Work

2. Common Answer Patterns in Governance Questions

PatternTypical score
Follows the governance process: review, assess, escalate to the Architecture Board, decide, recordUsually the best answer
Right idea, but too rigid (e.g. forces immediate rework regardless of business impact) or skips a step such as recording the decisionOften second best
Informal handling: notes the issue but takes no governance actionOften third best
Changes the architecture or standard to match the deviation, or ignores itUsually the distractor
A dispensation is a legitimate tool, but only when granted by the Architecture Board, time-bound, and usually with conditions. "Just let the project continue" is not a dispensation.

4. Practice: Four Scenarios

The test below has four scenarios covering Phases E, F, G and H. Allow about 11 minutes each. After submitting, read the reasoning for every answer.

The takeaway

  • Phase E answers consolidate, group into work packages and use Transition Architectures that deliver value.
  • Phase F answers fit the plan to existing management frameworks and prioritize by value, cost and risk.
  • Phase G answers follow the governance process: compliance review, the Architecture Board, a time-bound dispensation if justified, and a record of the decision.
  • Phase H answers analyze impact, classify the change and use the stakeholder rules of thumb to decide whether a new cycle is needed.
