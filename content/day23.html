Days 23 and 24 switch from Foundation recall to Practitioner (Part 2) thinking. Part 2 doesn't ask what a term means; it asks what an architect should do in a realistic situation. Today covers how the Part 2 exam works, a method for answering scenario questions, and four practice scenarios on Phases A to D.

1. How the Part 2 Exam Works

8scenario questions
90minutes
24/40points to pass (60%)
Openbook (electronic copy)

Each question has a scenario, a question and four answers. Scoring is graded, not right or wrong:

AnswerPointsWhat it usually looks like
Best answer5Follows the TOGAF guidance for the right phase most completely and fits the scenario's constraints
Second best3The right idea, but incomplete, in the wrong order, or missing a key element such as stakeholders or governance
Third best1Partly relevant, but uses the wrong phase or technique, or misses the main point
Distractor0Contradicts TOGAF, skips phases, or ignores the scenario
Because the open book is built into the exam, you don't need to memorize every step. You do need to know where to look: each ADM phase chapter lists its objectives, inputs, steps and outputs.

2. A Method for Scenario Questions

  1. Read the question first. It tells you what decision you are being asked to make.
  2. Identify the phase or topic. Is this Phase A scoping, Phase B modelling, Phase G governance?
  3. Read the scenario for facts and constraints: stakeholders, deadlines, existing frameworks, principles, risks.
  4. Look up the phase in the open book and compare each answer against its steps and outputs.
  5. Rank all four answers, not just the best one. A good second choice still earns 3 points.
Signs of a weak answer: jumping straight to products or vendors, skipping or reordering phases, ignoring stakeholders, bypassing governance, or treating the ADM as a rigid checklist that ignores the scenario.

3. What Strong Answers Contain, Phases A–D

PhaseA strong answer usually mentions
AStakeholders and concerns · business goals, drivers and constraints · capability and readiness assessment · scope · principles · Architecture Vision (often via business scenarios) · risks · Statement of Architecture Work approval
BViewpoints and reference models · Baseline and Target (choosing the sensible order) · capabilities and value streams · gap analysis · candidate roadmap components · impacts · stakeholder review
CData: data models, ownership, governance, security and migration. Applications: portfolio, functions supported, interactions, rationalization. Interoperability and gap analysis
DThe TRM and the Standards Library · technology services the applications need · performance, availability, location, security · controlling technical diversity · gap analysis and roadmap components

4. Practice: Four Scenarios

The test below has four scenarios, one for each of Phases A, B, C and D. Allow about 11 minutes each, the real exam's pace. After submitting, read the reasoning for every answer: understanding why the 3-point and 1-point answers fall short matters as much as recognizing the best one.

The takeaway

  • Part 2: 8 scenarios, 90 minutes, open book, graded 5 / 3 / 1 / 0, pass at 24 of 40.
  • Read the question first, identify the phase, then check each answer against that phase's steps and outputs.
  • The best answer is usually the most complete, TOGAF-aligned approach for the right phase that fits the scenario's constraints.
  • Watch for answers that skip phases, jump to products, ignore stakeholders or bypass governance.
