Deliverables and building blocks are among the most tested topics in Part 1. Today goes beyond the definitions from Day 2: what the main deliverables contain, how the Architecture Definition Document differs from the Architecture Requirements Specification, and how building blocks evolve through the ADM.

1. Quick Recap

TermOne-line definition
DeliverableA work product that is contractually specified, then formally reviewed, agreed and signed off
ArtifactA more granular work product describing one aspect of the architecture: a catalog, matrix or diagram
Building blockA (potentially reusable) component of enterprise capability that can be combined with others

3. Architecture Definition Document vs Architecture Requirements Specification

These two are easy to confuse. They are developed side by side and finalized together in Phase F.

Architecture Definition Document (ADD)Architecture Requirements Specification (ARS)
NatureQualitative: describes the architectureQuantitative: states measurable criteria
AudienceStakeholders who need to understand the solutionImplementation projects that must comply
Typical contentsScope; goals, objectives and constraints; principles; Baseline and Target for each domain; rationale; gap analysis; impact assessmentSuccess measures; architecture requirements; business and application service contracts; implementation guidelines and specifications; standards; interoperability requirements; constraints; assumptions
Memory hook: the ADD says what the architecture looks like; the ARS says what an implementation must achieve.

4. Building Blocks in Depth

What goes into the specifications

ABB specification includesSBB specification includes
  • Fundamental functionality and attributes, including security and manageability
  • Interfaces: chosen set, supplied
  • Interoperability and relationships with other building blocks
  • Dependent building blocks and the functionality they require
  • Map to business and organizational entities and policies
  • Specific functionality and attributes
  • Interfaces: the implemented set
  • Required SBBs, with their functionality and names of their interfaces
  • Mapping to the IT topology and operational policies
  • Shared attributes such as security, manageability and scalability
  • Performance and configurability; design drivers and constraints
  • Relationships between the SBBs and the ABBs they implement

How building blocks evolve through the ADM

Phase Abuilding blocks identified at a high level in the vision
↓ refined
Phases B, C, DABBs defined for each domain
↓ realized
Phases E, FSBBs identified: build, buy or reuse
↓ delivered
Phase GSBBs implemented and governed

ABBs relate to the Architecture Continuum, and SBBs relate to the Solutions Continuum (Day 19).

Good building block design

  • Choose a sensible granularity: too fine creates complexity, too coarse limits reuse.
  • Group functionality that naturally belongs together and that can be delivered and replaced as a unit.
  • Define clear, published interfaces so blocks can be swapped and combined.
  • Look for existing building blocks in the Architecture Repository before creating new ones.

The takeaway

  • Know which phase creates each key deliverable: Statement of Architecture Work in A, Implementation Governance Model in F, Compliance Assessment in G.
  • The ADD is qualitative (describes the architecture); the ARS is quantitative (criteria implementations must meet).
  • ABB specs describe required functionality and interfaces; SBB specs describe the implemented functionality, interfaces, topology and performance.
  • Building blocks go from high level in A, to ABBs in B–D, to SBBs in E–F, to implementation in G.
