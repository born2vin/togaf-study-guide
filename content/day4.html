1. Purpose of Phase A

Phase A is the initial phase of each ADM cycle. It defines the scope, identifies the stakeholders, creates the Architecture Vision, and obtains approval to proceed with the architecture work.

Phase A is started by a Request for Architecture Work, sent by the sponsoring organization to the architecture organization. It ends when the Statement of Architecture Work is approved.

2. Objectives

  • Develop a high-level, aspirational vision of the capabilities and business value to be delivered by the proposed EA.
  • Obtain approval for a Statement of Architecture Work that defines a programme of work to develop and deploy the architecture described in the Architecture Vision.

3. Inputs

  • Request for Architecture Work (the key trigger)
  • Business principles, business goals and business drivers
  • Organizational Model for EA and Tailored Architecture Framework (from the Preliminary Phase), including Architecture Principles
  • A populated Architecture Repository: existing architecture documentation, reference models and standards

4. Steps

  1. Establish the architecture project (within the enterprise's project management framework)
  2. Identify stakeholders, concerns and business requirements
  3. Confirm and elaborate business goals, drivers and constraints
  4. Evaluate capabilities (baseline and target capability levels)
  5. Assess readiness for business transformation
  6. Define scope
  7. Confirm and elaborate Architecture Principles, including business principles
  8. Develop the Architecture Vision
  9. Define the Target Architecture value propositions and KPIs
  10. Identify business transformation risks and mitigation activities
  11. Develop the Statement of Architecture Work and secure approval
Risk in Phase A: TOGAF classifies risk as initial level of risk (before mitigation) and residual level of risk (after mitigation). Risks are identified in Phase A and managed throughout the cycle.
Business Transformation Readiness Assessment evaluates how ready the organization is to accept change, using readiness factors such as vision, desire and willingness, need, business case, funding, sponsorship, governance, accountability, IT capacity and ability to implement.

5. Defining Scope

TOGAF defines the scope of an architecture effort across four dimensions:

DimensionQuestion it answers
BreadthWhich parts of the enterprise are covered? (the full enterprise, a business unit, a function)
DepthHow much detail is needed?
Time periodHow far into the future does the architecture look, and how many transition steps are needed?
Architecture domainsWhich of Business, Data, Application and Technology are included?

Scope is shaped by practical constraints such as time, cost, available skills and the organization's readiness.

6. The Architecture Vision and the Statement of Architecture Work

Architecture Vision

A high-level, aspirational description of the Target Architecture and the business value it will deliver. It acts as the architect's "elevator pitch": a tool to explain the benefits to stakeholders and win their support.
  • It is developed at a high level, often using a Solution Concept diagram.
  • Business Scenarios are a key technique for discovering and documenting the business requirements behind the vision.
  • It includes value propositions and KPIs that show how success will be measured.

Statement of Architecture Work

The formal agreement between the sponsor and the architecture organization. It defines the scope and approach for completing the architecture project, and it is the document against which successful execution is measured.

Typical contents: title, architecture project request and background, project description and scope, overview of the Architecture Vision, specific change of scope procedures, roles and responsibilities, architecture approach, work plan, risks and mitigation, acceptance criteria and procedures, schedule, and approvals.

7. Outputs

  • Approved Statement of Architecture Work
  • Refined statements of business principles, goals and drivers
  • Confirmed Architecture Principles
  • Capability Assessment
  • Tailored Architecture Framework (refined for this project)
  • Architecture Vision
  • Draft Architecture Definition Document, with high-level baseline and target for Business, Data, Application and Technology
  • Communications Plan
  • Additional content for the Architecture Repository

The takeaway

  • Phase A starts with a Request for Architecture Work and ends with an approved Statement of Architecture Work.
  • It identifies stakeholders and concerns, confirms goals, drivers and principles, and defines scope by breadth, depth, time period and domains.
  • The Architecture Vision is a high-level, aspirational "elevator pitch", often supported by Business Scenarios and a Solution Concept diagram.
  • It also covers capability assessment, transformation readiness, value propositions and KPIs, and risk (initial and residual).
  • Other outputs include the draft Architecture Definition Document and the Communications Plan.
