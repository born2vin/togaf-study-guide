Day 11 introduced governance through Phase G. Today looks at architecture governance as a permanent capability: where it sits in the enterprise, the Architecture Board, Architecture Contracts and the Architecture Compliance review process.

1. What Architecture Governance Is

Architecture governance is the practice and orientation by which enterprise architectures and other architectures are managed and controlled at an enterprise-wide level.

It includes implementing a system of controls over the creation and monitoring of architecture components, ensuring compliance with internal and external standards and regulatory obligations, and establishing processes for accountability.

2. Characteristics of Good Governance

CharacteristicMeaning
DisciplineAll parties commit to the procedures, processes and authority structures
TransparencyActions and decision support are open to inspection
IndependenceProcesses and decisions minimize conflicts of interest
AccountabilityGroups that take actions or make decisions are authorized and answerable for them
ResponsibilityEach party acts responsibly towards the organization and its stakeholders
FairnessNo party gains an unfair advantage from decisions or actions
Memory hook: "DTI-ARF": Discipline, Transparency, Independence, Accountability, Responsibility, Fairness.

3. The Architecture Governance Framework

TOGAF describes governance through process, content and context: the processes that do the work, the content they manage (held in the Governance Repository), and the organizational context they operate in. The key governance processes are:

ProcessPurpose
Policy management and take-onBring new or changed architecture policies, standards and contracts into the governance framework
ComplianceAssess projects against the architecture through compliance reviews
DispensationHandle cases where a project cannot comply, granting time-bound exceptions
Monitoring and reportingTrack the performance of governance and report on it
Business controlEnsure business processes and policies are followed
Environment managementKeep the governance environment and repository running effectively

4. The Architecture Board

The Architecture Board is a cross-organizational body that oversees the implementation of architecture governance. Its responsibilities include:

  • Consistency between sub-architectures
  • Identifying reusable components
  • Flexibility of the EA, so it can meet changing business needs
  • Enforcing compliance and granting dispensations
  • Improving the maturity of the architecture discipline
  • Ensuring a discipline-based approach to architecture
  • Decision-making and conflict resolution, with escalation where needed
TOGAF suggests a board of around four or five permanent members, and no more than ten, so it can make decisions effectively. Members should represent the key stakeholders and be senior enough to make binding decisions.

5. Architecture Contracts

An Architecture Contract is a joint agreement between development partners and sponsors on the deliverables, quality and fitness-for-purpose of an architecture. Contracts are used:

  • between the architecture function and the sponsor: the Statement of Architecture Work works as this kind of agreement;
  • between the architecture function and development partners: to make sure implementation conforms to the architecture;
  • between the architecture function and business users: to agree on the architecture as it affects them.

Contracts are prepared in Phase F, signed in Phase G and kept in the Governance Repository.

6. Architecture Compliance Reviews

Purpose

  • Catch errors in a project early, when they are cheaper to fix
  • Ensure best practice is applied to architecture work
  • Identify where enterprise standards may need to change
  • Identify application-specific services that could become shared infrastructure
  • Give management a view of the technical readiness of projects

Roles

Architecture Board · Project Leader · Customer · Lead Enterprise Architect · Architect · Project Principals

Process (simplified)

  1. Request the review
  2. Identify the responsible parts of the organization
  3. Identify the Lead Enterprise Architect and determine the scope of the review
  4. Tailor the checklists to the project
  5. Schedule the review meeting and interview the project principals
  6. Analyze the completed checklists and prepare the report
  7. Present the report, then accept and sign off the review
  8. Send the assessment report to the Architecture Board
Compliance reviews are not only held at the end of a project. They can be scheduled at key points: project initiation, initial design, major design changes, or whenever the Board requests one.

The takeaway

  • Governance hierarchy: corporate → technology → IT → architecture.
  • Six characteristics: Discipline, Transparency, Independence, Accountability, Responsibility, Fairness.
  • Governance processes: policy management, compliance, dispensation, monitoring and reporting, business control, environment management.
  • The Architecture Board ensures consistency, reuse and compliance, grants dispensations and resolves conflicts.
  • Architecture Contracts agree deliverables, quality and fitness-for-purpose; compliance reviews check projects at key points.
