The solution is live. The ADM is not over, though: Phase H keeps the architecture relevant as the business changes, and Requirements Management sits at the centre of every phase. Together they close the loop of the ADM cycle.

1. Purpose of Phase H

Phase H establishes procedures for managing change to the new architecture. It monitors business and technology changes and decides whether they can be absorbed or require a new ADM cycle.

2. Phase H Objectives

  • Ensure that the architecture lifecycle is maintained.
  • Ensure that the Architecture Governance Framework is executed.
  • Ensure that the enterprise's Architecture Capability meets current requirements.

3. Phase H Steps

  1. Establish the value realization process
  2. Deploy monitoring tools
  3. Manage risks
  4. Provide analysis for architecture change management
  5. Develop change requirements to meet performance targets
  6. Manage the governance process
  7. Activate the process to implement change

4. Drivers and Types of Change

Drivers for change

  • Strategic, top-down directed change to enhance or create new capability
  • Bottom-up changes to correct or enhance capability for operations and maintenance
  • Lessons learned from implementing the architecture (from Phases F and G)

Three classes of change

ClassHow it is handled
Simplification changeCan normally be handled through change management techniques
Incremental changeMay be handled through change management, or may need partial re-architecting, depending on its nature
Re-architecting changeRequires putting the whole architecture through the ADM cycle again

TOGAF's rules of thumb

  • If the change impacts two or more stakeholders, it is likely to need an architecture redesign and re-entry to the ADM.
  • If the change impacts only one stakeholder, it is more likely a candidate for change management.
  • If the change can be allowed under a dispensation, it is more likely a candidate for change management.
A re-architecting change typically triggers a new Request for Architecture Work, which starts a new ADM cycle at Phase A.

5. Phase H Outputs

  • Architecture updates (for maintenance changes)
  • Changes to the architecture framework and principles (for maintenance changes)
  • New Request for Architecture Work, to start another ADM cycle
  • Updated Statement of Architecture Work and Architecture Contract, if needed
  • Updated Compliance Assessments

6. Requirements Management

Requirements Management is the process, at the centre of the ADM, for managing architecture requirements throughout the ADM cycle.

Objectives

  • Ensure the Requirements Management process is sustained and operates for all relevant ADM phases.
  • Manage architecture requirements identified during any execution of the ADM cycle or a phase.
  • Ensure that relevant architecture requirements are available for use by each phase as it is executed.
Requirements Management does not itself dispose of, address or prioritize requirements. That is done within the relevant ADM phase. Requirements Management is the dynamic process that stores and supplies them.

How it works

  • Requirements are identified in a phase, then baselined and stored in the Requirements Repository.
  • Changes to requirements are identified and assessed through a Requirements Impact Assessment, which considers the impact on the current phase and on earlier phases.
  • The Architecture Requirements Specification is the main output that records requirements.
  • Business Scenarios are a useful technique for discovering and documenting requirements, and a template such as Volere can be used to record them.

The takeaway

  • Phase H keeps the architecture current: it maintains the lifecycle, executes governance, and keeps the Architecture Capability fit for purpose.
  • Change classes: Simplification (change management), Incremental (either), Re-architecting (new ADM cycle).
  • Changes affecting two or more stakeholders usually need redesign; changes affecting one, or allowed under a dispensation, usually go through change management.
  • Requirements Management is at the centre of the ADM; it stores and supplies requirements, but phases prioritize and address them.
  • Key items: Requirements Repository, Requirements Impact Assessment, and the Architecture Requirements Specification.
