Days 3–13 walked through the ADM one phase at a time. In practice the ADM is never run as a single straight line. Today covers how TOGAF expects you to apply it: iterating, working at different levels of the enterprise, partitioning the work, and handling cross-cutting concerns such as security.

1. The ADM Is Applied, Not Just Followed

The ADM is a generic method. To be useful it must be adapted to the enterprise: its scope, its maturity, the level of detail needed, and the other frameworks and methods already in use.

Applying the ADM means answering a few practical questions for every engagement:

  • How many times should we go round the cycle, and which phases need repeating?
  • At what level are we working: the whole enterprise, a segment, or a single capability?
  • How do we divide a large architecture into manageable pieces?
  • How do concerns that cut across every phase, such as security and risk, get handled?

2. Iteration in the ADM

TOGAF supports iteration at three levels:

  • Cycling around the whole ADM: one completed cycle leads directly into the next, often triggered by Phase H.
  • Iterating between phases: for example, returning from Phase C to Phase B when a new business requirement appears.
  • Cycling around a single phase: repeating a phase to add detail or resolve open issues.

The four iteration cycles

TOGAF groups the phases into four iteration cycles. Each can be repeated on its own before moving on:

Iteration cyclePhasesPurpose
Architecture CapabilityPreliminary and AEstablish or adjust the architecture approach, principles, scope, vision and governance
Architecture DevelopmentB, C and DCreate the architecture content across the domains
Transition PlanningE and FPlan the formal change initiatives and the roadmap
Architecture GovernanceG and HGovern implementation and manage change to the architecture
When deciding how to iterate, consider the formality and level of detail needed, the scope of each iteration, and how stakeholders will review the results. Small, focused iterations get feedback earlier.

3. Baseline First or Target First

Inside the Architecture Development iteration, TOGAF describes two ways of sequencing the work:

ApproachHow it worksWhen it fits
Baseline FirstDescribe the current state, find the problems, then design the targetThe baseline is not well understood, or change will be incremental
Target FirstDescribe the target in detail, then describe only as much of the baseline as needed to identify the gapsThe target is substantially different from today, or the baseline is already well documented

4. Applying the ADM at Different Levels

An enterprise is too large to describe in one architecture. TOGAF organizes the Architecture Landscape into three levels of granularity:

LevelWhat it describesTypical use
Strategic ArchitectureA long-term, summary view of the whole enterpriseSetting direction; executive decisions

Capability ArchitectureA detailed view of how a particular capability or solution will be supportedGuiding projects and change increments

A separate ADM cycle can be run at each level. A higher-level cycle often spawns lower-level cycles: for example, Phase E or F of a strategic cycle may identify segments that need their own, more detailed architecture work.

5. Architecture Partitioning

Because the enterprise is so large and complex, TOGAF recommends partitioning it into smaller architectures that can be developed and governed separately. Common ways to partition include:

  • Subject matter (breadth): which part of the enterprise, such as a business unit or domain
  • Time: the baseline, a transition state, or the target
  • Level of detail (depth): strategic, segment or capability
  • Viewpoint: the stakeholder perspective being addressed

Partitions must still fit together. Shared standards, the Architecture Repository and architecture governance keep them consistent and resolve conflicts between them.

6. Security and Other Cross-Cutting Concerns

Security is not a separate domain or phase. It is a concern addressed throughout the ADM:

PhaseTypical security activity
PreliminaryAgree security policies, principles and the security governance approach
AIdentify security stakeholders and concerns; assess business risk
B, C, DDefine security requirements and controls for business, data, applications and technology
E, FPlan security work packages and assess implementation risk
GCheck that implementations meet security requirements
HRespond to new threats and changes in regulation

In the 10th Edition, detailed guidance on security and risk is published as a TOGAF Series Guide.

7. Architectural Styles and Agile

The ADM is style-neutral: it can be used for service-oriented, digital, cloud or microservice architectures alike. It also supports agile delivery. Typically, architecture at the strategic and segment levels sets direction and guardrails, while agile teams deliver capability increments in short iterations.

The ADM is not a waterfall method. Exam options claiming that each phase must be fully completed before the next, or that TOGAF cannot be used with agile, are wrong.

The takeaway

  • The ADM is adapted to each engagement and supports iteration around the cycle, between phases and within a phase.
  • Four iteration cycles: Capability (Prelim, A), Development (B–D), Transition Planning (E, F), Governance (G, H).
  • Baseline First suits incremental change; Target First suits radical change.
  • Landscape levels: Strategic, Segment, Capability. Higher-level cycles can spawn lower-level ones.
  • Partition large architectures by subject matter, time, level of detail or viewpoint, and keep them consistent through governance.
  • Security is cross-cutting, and the ADM works with any style, including agile.
