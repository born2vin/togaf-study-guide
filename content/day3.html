1. Purpose of the Preliminary Phase

The Preliminary Phase prepares the organization for successful Enterprise Architecture. It answers the where, what, why, who and how of architecture in the enterprise, and it establishes the Architecture Capability that will run the ADM.
  • Where: the scope of the enterprise and the organizations affected.
  • What and why: the business drivers and what EA must achieve.
  • Who: the EA team, sponsors, and the governance bodies such as the Architecture Board.
  • How: the tailored framework, the principles, the tools, and the governance process.

It sits outside the A–H cycle. It is usually done once to set up the capability, then revisited when the organization or its EA practice changes significantly.

2. Objectives

TOGAF groups the objectives into two areas:

Determine the Architecture Capability desiredEstablish the Architecture Capability
  • Review the organizational context for EA
  • Identify and scope the parts of the enterprise affected
  • Identify the frameworks, methods and processes already in use that interact with EA
  • Establish a target for Architecture Capability maturity
  • Define and establish the Organizational Model for EA
  • Define and establish the detailed process and resources for architecture governance
  • Select and implement tools that support the capability
  • Define the Architecture Principles

3. Inputs

  • The TOGAF Library and other architecture frameworks
  • Board strategies, business plans, business strategy and IT strategy
  • Business principles, goals and drivers
  • Major frameworks already operating in the business (e.g. project or portfolio management, ITIL, COBIT)
  • Governance and legal frameworks, including architecture governance strategy
  • Partnership and contract agreements
  • Any existing Organizational Model for EA, architecture framework, Architecture Principles and Architecture Repository

4. Steps

  1. Scope the enterprise organizations impacted
  2. Confirm governance and support frameworks
  3. Define and establish the EA team and organization
  4. Identify and establish Architecture Principles
  5. Tailor the TOGAF framework and any other selected frameworks
  6. Develop strategy and implementation plans for tools and techniques
Scoping the enterprise: TOGAF distinguishes the core enterprise (units directly affected), the soft enterprise (units that will see change but are not directly part of it), the extended enterprise (partners outside the organization) and communities (stakeholder groups with common purpose).

5. Outputs

  • Organizational Model for Enterprise Architecture: scope of the organizations affected, maturity assessment and gaps, roles and responsibilities for the architecture team, constraints on architecture work, budget requirements, governance and support strategy.
  • Tailored Architecture Framework: the tailored architecture method and content, the Architecture Principles, configured and deployed tools, and interfaces with governance models and other frameworks.
  • An initial Architecture Repository, populated with framework content
  • Restatement of, or reference to, business principles, goals and drivers
  • A Request for Architecture Work (optional; it triggers Phase A)
  • An Architecture Governance Framework
Exam trap: the Statement of Architecture Work is not a Preliminary output. It is produced and approved in Phase A. The Preliminary Phase may produce the Request for Architecture Work that starts Phase A.

6. Tailoring TOGAF

TOGAF is meant to be adapted to the organization. Tailoring happens in three areas:

AreaWhat is adapted
TerminologyUse terms the organization already understands, so architecture is easy to communicate
ProcessAdd, remove, merge or reorder ADM phases and steps; align with existing processes such as project management and procurement
ContentChoose which deliverables and artifacts to produce, and adapt the content metamodel

The Preliminary Phase must also make sure TOGAF fits with other frameworks in use, such as business planning, portfolio management, operations management and solution development.

7. Architecture Principles

Principles are general rules and guidelines, intended to be enduring and seldom amended, that inform and support the way an organization sets about fulfilling its mission.

Principle hierarchy

Enterprise principleswhole organization
↓ inform
IT principlesuse of IT
↓ inform
Architecture principlesarchitecture work

Architecture Principles govern both the architecture process (how architecture is developed) and the architecture itself (how it is implemented). They are established in the Preliminary Phase and confirmed and elaborated in Phase A.

The four components of a principle

ComponentWhat it contains
NameA short, memorable label that captures the essence of the rule. It should avoid naming specific technology platforms.
StatementA clear, unambiguous statement of the fundamental rule
RationaleThe business benefits of following the principle, in business terms, and how it relates to other principles
ImplicationsThe requirements for carrying out the principle, for both the business and IT: resources, costs, and activities or tasks
Memory hook: "NSRI". Rationale answers why (benefits). Implications answer what it will take (impact and cost).

The five criteria for a good set of principles

CriterionMeaning
UnderstandableQuickly grasped by people across the organization; the intent is clear and unambiguous
RobustEnable good-quality decisions; precise enough to support consistent decision-making in complex, potentially controversial situations; enforceable
CompleteEvery potentially important principle for governing IT and information management is defined; they cover every situation perceived
ConsistentStrict adherence to one principle may need a loose interpretation of another, so principles must allow a balance of interpretations; they should not contradict each other
StableEnduring, yet able to accommodate change through an amendment process
Memory hook: "U-R-C-C-S" ("You Are Creating Consistent Standards").

Primacy of Principles says the principles apply to all organizations within the enterprise. It is the principle that gives all the others their authority.

The takeaway

  • The Preliminary Phase prepares the organization: the where, what, why, who and how of EA.
  • Its six steps: scope the organizations, confirm governance, set up the EA team, establish principles, tailor TOGAF, and plan tools.
  • Key outputs: Organizational Model for EA, Tailored Architecture Framework (which includes the principles), initial Architecture Repository, and optionally a Request for Architecture Work.
  • Tailoring covers terminology, process and content.
  • A principle has four parts: Name, Statement, Rationale, Implications.
  • A good set of principles is Understandable, Robust, Complete, Consistent and Stable.
