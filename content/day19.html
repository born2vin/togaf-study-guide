An architecture practice produces a lot of material, and much of it can be reused. Today covers the two ideas TOGAF uses to organize it: the Enterprise Continuum, a way of classifying assets, and the Architecture Repository, where they are stored. Both are part of the Architecture Content document in the 10th Edition.

1. The Enterprise Continuum

The Enterprise Continuum is a view of the Architecture Repository that provides methods for classifying architecture and solution artifacts, both internal and external, as they evolve from generic Foundation Architectures to Organization-Specific Architectures.

It helps architects communicate clearly about what kind of asset they are discussing, and it encourages reuse: start from the most generic suitable asset and specialize it, rather than building from scratch. It contains two complementary continua.

2. The Architecture Continuum

Level (generic → specific)What it containsExample
Foundation ArchitectureGeneric components, principles and guidelines that all other architectures are built onThe TOGAF Technical Reference Model (TRM)
Common Systems ArchitecturesFoundation services selected and integrated into reusable architectures for a problem domainSecurity, management or networking architectures; the III-RM
Industry ArchitecturesCommon systems components combined with industry-specific components for a vertical sectorIndustry reference models for retail, energy or banking
Organization-Specific ArchitecturesArchitectures that guide the final deployment of solutions for one enterpriseYour bank's Target Architecture

3. The Solutions Continuum

The Solutions Continuum holds the implementations that correspond to each level of the Architecture Continuum:

LevelWhat it contains
Foundation SolutionsHighly generic concepts, tools, products, services and components, such as programming languages and operating systems
Common Systems SolutionsImplementations of Common Systems Architectures, often branded products and services
Industry SolutionsImplementations of Industry Architectures, packaged for a sector and ready to tailor
Organization-Specific SolutionsImplementations of Organization-Specific Architectures, with the most unique content
Architecture ContinuumABBs; what is needed
↓ guides and supports, at each level
Solutions ContinuumSBBs; how it is implemented
Reading direction: the left is generic, the right is specific. Architects look to the left for reusable assets and move right as they specialize them for the enterprise. Moving left is generalization: extracting reusable assets from specific work.

4. The Architecture Repository

The Architecture Repository stores the different classes of architectural output. In the 10th Edition it has these components:

ComponentWhat it holds
Architecture MetamodelThe organization's tailored use of the framework, including the method and content metamodel
Architecture CapabilityThe parameters, structures and processes that support governance of the repository
Architecture LandscapeViews of the building blocks in use, or planned, at particular points in time
Standards LibraryThe standards new architectures must comply with: industry standards, selected products, shared services
Reference LibraryGuidelines, templates, patterns and reference material that speed up architecture work
Governance RepositoryA record of governance activity across the enterprise
Architecture Requirements RepositoryAll authorized architecture requirements agreed with the Architecture Board
Solutions LandscapeThe planned or deployed SBBs that support the Architecture Landscape
Earlier editions used different names: Standards Information Base (now Standards Library) and Governance Log (now Governance Repository). Recognize both, but use the 10th Edition names.

5. The Architecture Landscape

The landscape is held at three levels of granularity:

  • Strategic Architectures: long-term, enterprise-wide summary views
  • Segment Architectures: more detailed operating models for areas of the enterprise
  • Capability Architectures: detailed views of how a unit of capability is supported

The takeaway

  • The Enterprise Continuum is a view of the repository for classifying assets from generic to specific.
  • Architecture Continuum: Foundation → Common Systems → Industry → Organization-Specific. The Solutions Continuum mirrors it with implementations.
  • The Architecture Continuum guides the Solutions Continuum at each level; ABBs ↔ Architecture, SBBs ↔ Solutions.
  • Repository components: Metamodel, Capability, Landscape, Standards Library, Reference Library, Governance Repository, Requirements Repository, Solutions Landscape.
  • Standards move from Proposed → Provisional → Standard → Phasing-Out → Retired.
