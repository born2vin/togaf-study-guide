1. What is Enterprise Architecture?

Enterprise: the highest level of description of an organization, typically covering all its missions and functions. An enterprise can span several organizations, such as a partner ecosystem or a government department and its agencies. It can also be a smaller scope, like a single division.
Architecture has two definitions in TOGAF:
  1. (from ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010) The fundamental concepts or properties of a system in its environment, embodied in its elements, its relationships, and the principles of its design and evolution.
  2. The structure of components, their relationships, and the principles and guidelines governing their design and evolution over time.

Purpose of EA: Many organizations have fragmented, siloed processes and systems. EA aims to turn these into an integrated environment that responds to change and supports business strategy. It connects strategy to execution.

2. What is TOGAF?

  • TOGAF stands for The Open Group Architecture Framework.
  • It is an open, vendor-neutral standard maintained by The Open Group.
  • It grew out of the US DoD's TAFIM, and version 1 appeared in 1995.
  • An architecture framework is a foundational structure (tools, methods, a common vocabulary) for developing a broad range of different architectures.
  • TOGAF's centrepiece is the Architecture Development Method (ADM), a proven, repeatable process for developing architectures.
  • TOGAF is designed to be tailored. It is not meant to be applied rigidly "as is."

3. The Four Architecture Domains

DomainDescribes
Business ArchitectureBusiness strategy, governance, organization and key business processes
Data ArchitectureLogical and physical data assets and data management resources
Application ArchitectureIndividual applications, their interactions, and their relationships to business processes
Technology ArchitectureSoftware and hardware capabilities needed to deploy business, data and application services (infrastructure, middleware, networks, processing)

Data and Application together form the Information Systems Architectures, which are both handled in Phase C.

4. The ADM at a Glance

The ADM is iterative and cyclical. It is often drawn as a "crop circle":

PreliminaryPrepare the organization. Define the framework, principles and EA capability.
A. Architecture VisionScope, stakeholders, high-level vision, and the Statement of Architecture Work.
B. Business ArchitectureBaseline and target business architecture; gap analysis.
C. Information SystemsData and Application Architectures.
D. Technology ArchitectureInfrastructure and platform architecture.
E. Opportunities & SolutionsIdentify work packages and transition architectures.
F. Migration PlanningA prioritised Implementation and Migration Plan.
G. Implementation GovernanceEnsure implementations conform to the architecture.
H. Architecture Change MgmtManage change and decide when a new cycle is needed.
Requirements Management (centre)A continuous process that feeds and is fed by every phase.

Iteration happens at several levels:

  • around the whole cycle;
  • between phases, for example cycling back through B–D;
  • within a single phase.

5. Structure of the TOGAF Standard, 10th Edition

TOGAF 10 introduced a modular structure with two parts.

TOGAF Fundamental Content is the stable core:
  1. Introduction and Core Concepts
  2. Architecture Development Method
  3. ADM Techniques
  4. Applying the ADM
  5. Architecture Content
  6. Enterprise Architecture Capability and Governance
TOGAF Series Guides give context-specific guidance, for example on business capabilities, value streams, the TRM/III-RM reference models, digital, agile and security. They can evolve faster than the core.
⚠️ Exam trap: the "seven Parts" layout, with the TRM as its own Part, belongs to TOGAF 9.x, not TOGAF 10.

6. Other Core Concepts (previewed; covered in depth later)

  • Enterprise Continuum: a view of the Architecture Repository for classifying architecture and solution assets. Assets range from generic (Foundation) to organization-specific.
  • Architecture Repository: stores the architecture outputs across the enterprise.
  • Architecture Capability: the people, processes and governance needed to run EA as an ongoing practice.

7. Benefits of EA / TOGAF

  • More effective and efficient business operations, such as lower costs and flexible processes.
  • More effective and efficient IT operations, such as lower change costs and better portability.
  • Better return on existing investment and reduced risk for future investment.
  • Faster, simpler and cheaper procurement.
  • A common language and a proven, open method, which reduces dependence on proprietary approaches.

📌 Day 1 Summary

TOGAF (The Open Group Architecture Framework) is an open, adaptable EA framework. It helps organizations move from fragmented systems to an integrated architecture aligned with strategy.

  • Its core is the iterative ADM: Preliminary, then Phases A–H, with Requirements Management at the centre.
  • It works across four domains: Business, Data, Application and Technology.
  • TOGAF 10 separates Fundamental Content from Series Guides.
  • Key definitions to memorise:
    • Enterprise (can span multiple organizations)
    • Architecture (ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010)
    • Architecture framework
